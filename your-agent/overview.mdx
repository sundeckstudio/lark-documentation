Your agent is already built. It has a name, a look and a voice, but it does not know your business yet. To answer well it needs context, and the better that context is, the better its answers.

Open **Knowledge sources** under Agent in your dashboard to add and manage everything your agent learns from. Each source shows a Ready badge once it can be used, or Needs review if it is held back.

## Ways to teach your agent

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Paste text" icon="paste">
    Paste your services, pricing, FAQs, team bios or process descriptions. Best for short, specific information you want your agent to get exactly right.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Upload a file" icon="file-arrow-up">
    Upload text, Markdown, HTML, JSON, PDF or Word (.docx) files, such as brochures, price lists and policy documents. Lark keeps the text and discards the original file.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Add a website page" icon="globe">
    Add a page from your website and Lark reads its text, up to 5 pages per source. Best for pages you already keep up to date, such as services, pricing and FAQs.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Connected tools (coming soon)" icon="plug">
    Soon your agent will also be able to learn from tools you connect, such as spreadsheets, documents and notes, so what it knows stays in step with what you keep there. Today your connected tools receive your leads. See [Integrations](/integrations/overview).
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## What your agent does when it does not know

Lark does not make things up. Your agent answers only from what you have added. If the answer is not there, it says so and offers to pass the visitor to you. A gap in your sources shows up as a missed question, not a wrong answer.

## Test before you publish

Use **Try agent** in your dashboard to ask your agent the questions your visitors ask. Try agent is separate from live conversations and does not use your conversation allowance. Test again after every change to your sources.

## Best practices

The quality of your agent's answers depends mostly on the sources you give it. Fix your sources first, because they cause most quality problems.

### Write plain, specific facts

Vague text produces vague answers. State what is true, with numbers and conditions.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Instead of" icon="xmark">
    Prices are probably around \$500 to \$1,000 depending on the project, and we usually take a few weeks.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Write" icon="check">
    Logo design costs \$800 and takes 5 business days. Brand packages start at \$2,500 and take 3 to 4 weeks.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

### Use sources with readable text

Lark reads text only. Images, videos and charts inside a file are not understood, so put important information into words. Scanned PDFs have very little readable text, so Lark holds them for review and does not use them in answers until you approve them. Paste the text or upload a text-based version instead.

### Handle pages that do not read well

Some website pages load their content in ways that are hard to read. If a page comes in with very little text, paste its content as text or upload it as a PDF.

### Cover what visitors really ask

Start with the questions you hear most: services, prices, process, timing, location and how to get in touch. Then look through your conversations for questions your agent could not answer and add the missing information. Choose suggested questions in [Personalize](/your-agent/persona-and-tone) that your sources answer well, because your agent answers them from your knowledge sources.

### Keep one clear version of each fact

When the same fact appears in several places with different wording, your agent can mix them up. Keep one clear version of each fact, and when prices, services or opening hours change, update the matching source so the old and new versions do not conflict.

### Correct a wrong answer

If your agent gives a wrong or incomplete answer, add a short text source that states the correct answer plainly, written the way a visitor would ask it. For example: "How long does a logo take? A logo takes 5 business days." Then test the question again. For more help, see [Wrong answers](/troubleshooting/wrong-answers).
